IT Ops Pain Points

🔗 The Integration Paradox

Bridging modern agile channels with rigid legacy core databases creates complex integration layers susceptible to unexpected cascading failures.

🌫️ Fragmented Visibility & Siloed Data

Monitoring tool sprawl and lack of end-to-end tracing leave blind spots, delaying incident identification and root-cause analysis.

🏚️ Compounding Technical Debt

Maintenance overhead from out-of-support legacy tech absorbs engineering capacity, delaying digital innovation timelines.

🌀 Environment Divergence & Drift

Managing configuration parity between on-premise and digital environments compromises disaster recovery consistency.

🧠 Knowledge Stratification

Operational logic concentrated among legacy specialists or isolated in modern teams—inhibiting unified knowledge sharing.

⚖️ Compliance vs. Performance Trade-offs

Balancing extensive log capture required by strict regulatory frameworks against storage costs and database performance constraints.

What Makes Them Unable to Sleep?

  • The Domino Effect

    No circuit breakers or automated failovers. A 5% packet loss can cascade into a complete multi-hour outage affecting millions of transactions.

  • Flying Blind — Severe Observability Gaps

    No unified tracing. Engineers manually SSH into servers, pulling fragmented logs under immense pressure during outages.

  • The Compliance Deficit Clock

    Logs deleted after 7 days vs. a 7-year regulatory mandate. Exposure to massive fines and unverifiable customer reimbursements.

  • "Zombie Applications" & Dependency Hell

    50+ apps in production, one third unmaintained with no business owners. No real-time dependency mapping — every maintenance window is a gamble.

  • Single Points of Failure in Human Capital

    Critical legacy logic locked in the heads of a few senior engineers. If they're unreachable, the whole team is paralysed.

  • The "WhatsApp Culture" of Incident Management

    Critical workarounds buried across unstructured, unsearchable WhatsApp groups — crisis response means frantically scrolling chat history at 2 AM.

Agents write ad-hoc wrapper code to capture ephemeral COBOL batch outputs or CICS circular buffers, mapping archaic data into uniform JSON schemas.
